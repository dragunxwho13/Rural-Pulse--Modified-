# Pitch outline (5 slides, 3 minutes)

1. **Problem.** In rural India, the first decision fails: where to go and how soon. Only 34.8% of rural households in one Karnataka study knew eSanjeevani existed; 29% of users hit network problems. People travel to the wrong place, unprepared.
2. **Insight.** Symptom checkers get the first diagnosis right only 19–38% of the time (systematic review). So don't build a diagnosis bot. Build a **care-navigation and appointment-preparation aid** that complements the 1.76 lakh Ayushman Arogya Mandirs and eSanjeevani.
3. **Product (live demo).** One flow for the patient, the ASHA and the doctor:
   - Speak or type in any of 22 Indian languages. Danger signs show 108 instantly, offline, before any AI.
   - The patient confirms a read-back. Urgency comes from versioned rules, and the system abstains when unsure.
   - "Do this now" first-aid steps, then the nearest government centre on the map with fastest and main-road routes.
   - The ASHA notifies the doctor, whose dashboard rings with symptoms and a prep list.
   - The doctor's prescription reaches the patient as a medicine card in their language, with pictures, audio and reminders. The pharmacy reserves the medicines.
4. **Why it's trustworthy.**
   - Rules decide; the LLM can only escalate.
   - Every facility shows its source and verification date; stale data is flagged.
   - Consent-based privacy.
   - The safety suite reports harm-weighted metrics, including the gap it found.
5. **What's next.** Clinician review of the protocol, a community language corpus, real NHM registry data, a silent-mode study, then a supervised pilot with ASHAs. Hypotheses to test: fewer wrong-facility trips and more complete referrals.

**Do not claim:** lives saved, reduced costs, clinical accuracy, "any dialect", or that OSM/listing data is verified. Claim what you built and what you will measure.
